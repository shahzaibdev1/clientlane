Who is this for?

You deliver work to clients.

Freelance designers, website builders, brand studios, and small creative teams can use one project space for the details that otherwise get scattered across messages.

Your client needs a clear next action.

The studio has its own workspace. The client follows a private project link to send responses, review versions, make decisions, and find approved files.

Use it when decisions slow delivery.

It is especially useful when you are chasing copy or assets, handling revision rounds, agreeing added scope, or preparing a final handover.

Agreement, with a clear record.

The $125.50 in this example is an accepted quote. Clientlane coordinates delivery and records scope and date decisions. It does not collect payments; invoicing and payment happen elsewhere.

OPTIONAL CLIENT ACCOUNTS

Bring your provider. Keep your projects together.

The video shows the private-link workflow, which remains available. Client accounts add a confirmed-email dashboard across the freelancers and agencies you already work with. Client accounts are free.

A studio invites its client.

Open a project and choose Invite client account. Preview/copy the seven-day invitation, or send it through configured email. The confirmed invited email must accept before the project appears in their client dashboard.

A client invites their provider.

Choose Invite your provider on the client dashboard, enter their existing provider email and a brief, and share the invitation. The provider accepts with that confirmed email; the project and client are created together in their owner workspace and linked to the originating client.

Work together with clear access.

Clients respond, upload, review versions, agree scope and deadlines, and open handover. Internal notes and studio controls stay private. One account can switch between client projects and a studio workspace. Account access and private links can be revoked independently.

Open your local client dashboard · Create a client account

Pending invitations grant no access. Recipients can decline and senders can revoke pending invitations. This brings existing working relationships into Clientlane; there is no provider marketplace or payment processing.

What happens in the example

Six steps. Two people. One project.

01 / GET ORIENTED

See what is waiting.

The designer opens the dashboard, then Field Coffee’s project. Missing inputs, the current deadline, and delivery decisions make the next action clear.

02 / GATHER INPUTS

Ask once, reply in context.

The designer requests the approved launch headline. Asha sends “Good coffee. Warm company.” through her client portal; the request becomes received.

03 / REVIEW A VERSION

Keep feedback with the work.

Asha adds feedback on version 1, then requests a clearer opening-hours section. The decision counts one revision round for that version.

04 / REVISE & APPROVE

Make the latest decision obvious.

The designer publishes version 2 with the requested change and a private file. Asha approves it. Earlier versions keep their own feedback and decisions.

05 / AGREE EXTRA WORK

Settle scope before starting.

Asha asks for drink illustrations. Northline publishes a USD $125.50 quote and proposes October 28. Asha accepts both; the project deadline updates.

06 / HAND OVER

Find the approved result.

Handover shows the latest approved delivery, its file, and an export. The illustration quote is an agreement for future work; the approved homepage is the work handed over.
